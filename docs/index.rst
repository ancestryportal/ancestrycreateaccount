Whether you are starting your genealogical journey from scratch or have received an AncestryDNA kit as a gift, understanding how to create an Ancestry account properly ensures you can access all the features and tools the platform offers. This comprehensive guide provides step-by-step instructions for creating a free Ancestry account, explains the different sign-up methods available, and covers important considerations such as password requirements, email verification, and what you can do with a free account.

Understanding Ancestry Account Creation
=======================================

Ancestry accounts are free to create and serve as your personal gateway to family history research, DNA results, and genealogical discoveries. When you create an Ancestry account, you become a "Registered Guest," which gives you access to free record collections, family tree building, and various other features. You can create a free Ancestry account by signing up with Google, Apple, or your email address .

Each person needs their own email address and account to use Ancestry . Each email address may only be connected to one Ancestry account, so if you share an email address with someone and it is already associated with an Ancestry account, one of you will need to use a different email address for Ancestry . If you don't have an email address, you can create one for free through providers like Gmail, Yahoo Mail, or Outlook .

If you purchase a DNA kit for another adult (or anyone other than your minor child), that person will need to register their kit on their own Ancestry account . After they have registered their kit on their account, they can invite you to manage their test . Adults who take an AncestryDNA test must register their own kits on their own accounts to agree to the Terms and Conditions and Privacy Statement .

Prerequisites for Creating an Ancestry Account
==============================================

Before you begin the account creation process, there are a few prerequisites to ensure a smooth experience. Proper preparation prevents issues and ensures you can access your account without unnecessary delays.

The primary prerequisite is having an active email address that is not already associated with an existing Ancestry account . If you don't have an email address, you can create one for free with providers like Gmail, Yahoo Mail, or Outlook . Each email address may be connected to only one Ancestry account . If you receive a message saying your email address is already in use, you can try signing in with that email address or sign up with a different email address .

If you share an email address with a spouse, partner, or someone else, one of you will need to use a different email address for your Ancestry account . You can create a new email address for free using any of the providers listed above.

For the email sign-up method, you will need to create a password that meets Ancestry's security requirements. Passwords are case-sensitive, should not be common or well-known, and must be at least eight characters long, contain at least one number, and contain at least one letter or special symbol .

Methods to Create an Ancestry Account
=====================================

Ancestry provides three methods to create a free account: signing up with Google, signing up with Apple, or signing up with your email address . Each method has its own advantages, and you can choose the one that best suits your preferences.

To create an Ancestry account, go to the account creation page. Then, choose one of the three sign-up methods described below.

Sign Up with Google
-------------------

To create your Ancestry account using Google, select the "Sign up with Google" option on the account creation page. You will be redirected to Google's sign-in page, where you will enter your Google account credentials. After successful authentication, Google provides Ancestry with your name, profile photo, and email address . Using this feature does not give Google access to your Ancestry account or any information beyond the fact that you are signing in to Ancestry . After connecting, you will enter your information and select "Create account" .

Sign Up with Apple
------------------

To create your Ancestry account using Apple, select the "Sign up with Apple" option. You will be prompted to enter your Apple ID and password. If asked, enter the Apple verification code and choose whether or not to trust this browser. You will then be given the option to set your preferred email privacy, including the option to "Hide my Email," which creates a unique Apple Relay Email address for your Ancestry account . Apple provides Ancestry with your name and an email address . After confirming, you will enter your information and select "Create account" .

If you choose "Hide my Email" when signing up with Apple, your account will be associated with a privaterelay.appleid.com email address created by Apple . If you later need to reset your password, you will need to find this Apple Relay Email to receive the reset code.

Sign Up with Email
------------------

To create your Ancestry account using an email address, select the "Sign up with email" option. You will be prompted to enter your first name, last name, and email address. You will need to re-enter the same email address for confirmation. Then, create a password that meets Ancestry's requirements: at least eight characters, containing at least one number, and containing at least one letter or special symbol . Re-enter the same password, then select "Create account" . After the account is created, Ancestry will send a confirmation email to the address you provided.

What You Get with a Free Ancestry Account
=========================================

When you create a free Ancestry account, you become a "Registered Guest." This free account gives you access to several features and tools that can help you start your family history research.

With a free account, you can access free record collections, free research guides, free charts and forms, and help in the Support Center . You can respond to messages and build family trees limited to your tree(s) and tree(s) you are invited to view . You also have access to Ancestry Hints limited to hints from free record collections . Free account holders can also access the powerful free family tree builder and the Ancestry hint system, which searches for potential matches in the background as new records are added .

Ancestry displays user-submitted information for free via its "Potential Father" and "Potential Mother" feature—a special type of hint that gathers all of the information about a mutual ancestor found in a public member tree . These hints should always be accepted with a degree of caution, but the tool enables you to copy the ancestor's full details across to your own tree, including the names of any records the researcher has saved.

To access premium records and additional features, you can start a membership at any time . Different memberships give you access to different records, including census, birth, marriage, death, and immigration records .

What You Can Do After Creating Your Account
===========================================

After creating your free Ancestry account, you can begin building your family tree, searching for records, and exploring your family history.

One of the first tasks a new registered guest will be prompted to undertake is to simply add a family tree: either by uploading a GEDCOM file or creating one from scratch . The key benefit lies in Ancestry's "hint" technology, which means that their search algorithms are constantly working in the background. Whenever Ancestry adds a new batch of records and there is a potential match, the hint system will flag it up .

Although it is not possible to carry out a manual search of Ancestry's public member tree collection without a subscription, you can still see hints relating to photos that others have chosen to share publicly. In these cases, the username of the person who has uploaded the photo will be displayed, allowing you to contact them via Ancestry's messaging facility and find out more .

If you have received an AncestryDNA kit as a gift, you can register your kit on your account . Each adult must register their kit on their own account, and you don't need a membership to see your AncestryDNA results . If someone else purchased the kit for you, they may have sent you an invitation to register it; you will need to create your own account or sign in to an existing one to complete the registration process .

If you are the recipient of a DNA kit invitation, click "Sign up to get started" in the invitation email to create a free Ancestry account . You don't need a membership, but a membership could help you make more discoveries .

Troubleshooting Common Account Creation Issues
==============================================

Even with careful preparation, users may occasionally encounter issues during the account creation process. Understanding common problems and their solutions helps resolve these issues quickly.

If you receive a message that your email address is already associated with an Ancestry account, you can try signing in using that email address and your Ancestry password (if you don't know your password, you can reset it) or sign up with a different email address . If you share an email with someone, one of you will need to use a different email address for Ancestry . You can create a new email address for free using providers like Gmail, Yahoo Mail, or Outlook .

If you are using the email sign-up method and are having trouble creating a password, ensure it meets the requirements: at least eight characters, containing at least one number, and containing at least one letter or special symbol . Passwords are case-sensitive and should not be common or well-known .

If you are signing up with Google or Apple and encounter issues, ensure you are using the correct credentials for those accounts. If you are using "Hide my Email" with Apple, be aware that your account will be associated with a privaterelay.appleid.com email address, which you will need to access if you need to reset your password later .
